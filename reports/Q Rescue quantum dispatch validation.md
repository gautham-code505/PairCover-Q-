# Narrow Q-Rescue before building anything quantum

Q-Rescue should proceed only after its objective is narrowed: as proposed, it is buildable but its headline claims ("predictive", "dynamic future coverage", QAOA benchmarked against greedy) are either unsupported or mathematically hollow. The decisive technical finding is that a per-ambulance coverage-loss penalty leaves the problem a linear assignment problem, which classical algorithms solve exactly in polynomial time, so the quantum framing has substance only if the coverage term contains pairwise interactions between different ambulances. The idea itself is not new: coverage-aware dispatch is established classical operations research since at least 2007, ambulance QUBOs solved with QAOA exist since 2022, and a public GitHub repository already carries the name "Q-Rescue". What a targeted, non-exhaustive search did not find is one QUBO combining multi-call assignment with a quadratic residual-coverage term, solved with QAOA and benchmarked against exact enumeration; that is a small formulation-level gap, not an algorithmic or performance contribution. The Round 1 deadline is verified as 10 October 2026, 11:59 PM IST, and Round 1 requires slides, a narrated three-minute video, a prototype and a public repository, so roughly one day remains for a notebook-scale build. No QAOA has been run by anyone in this research; every number below is either from a cited source or from the researchers' own brute-force checks on synthetic data. The official NAS 2026 guidelines PDF could not be opened, so every statement about its contents rests on press coverage.

Evidence labels used throughout: **[V-full]** source text read in full or in the relevant part; **[V-summary]** full page fetched but read through a summarising tool, so quoted wording is not character-exact; **[V-abstract]** abstract or landing page only; **[Snippet]** search-result summary only, source not opened; **[Unverified]** not confirmed. Claim types: **Verified fact**, **Inference** (researchers' reasoning), **Design** (a proposed modelling choice), **Computed** (researchers' brute-force enumeration on synthetic data, not a QAOA result), **Hypothesis** (untested).

## A. Executive verdict: proceed after narrowing or changing the objective

**Verdict: "Proceed after narrowing or changing the objective."**

The reasoning has five parts. First, the proposed objective does not justify a QUBO. A linear coverage penalty per dispatched ambulance simply adds a constant to each assignment cost, and the result is a rectangular linear assignment problem solved exactly by `scipy.optimize.linear_sum_assignment` ([SciPy docs](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.optimize.linear_sum_assignment.html)). Only a term that couples two different ambulances' departures makes the objective genuinely quadratic (Inference, both formulation notes agree). Second, the initial sizing works against the idea: with 3 ambulances and 4 calls, every near-optimal solution tends to dispatch all units, the coverage term becomes a constant, and it can change the answer only by leaving a present call unserved to protect hypothetical future ones (Computed on synthetic data; see section D). That is a safety-relevant policy choice, not an optimisation win. Third, "predictive" and "dynamic" are not supportable: there is no forecasting model, no data in hand, and a one-shot snapshot has no time axis. Fourth, greedy is a straw-man baseline at this size; the exact optimum is obtainable by enumerating at most a few thousand bitstrings, so the exact solver, not greedy, is the reference. Fifth, the classical literature's own evidence on coverage-aware dispatch is mixed: the best-known rule cut late arrivals by 18% while raising mean response time by 37%, and its authors expect decision makers to prefer closest-idle dispatch ([Jagtenberg et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10729-016-9368-0), V-abstract plus author manuscript V-full).

The narrowed project is a **static one-shot batch assignment with a pairwise residual-coverage term**, at 4 to 12 qubits, with QAOA hand-assembled from supported Qiskit components on a noiseless statevector simulator, validated against brute-force enumeration and compared with greedy, a linear-assignment baseline and uniform random sampling. Framed that way it is technically sound, the quantum component is real but explicitly non-advantageous, the distinctiveness is modest and honestly bounded, and it is buildable in a day as a notebook. The Streamlit dashboard, any learned forecaster and any use of Tamil Nadu data move out of Round 1.

**Patient-to-hospital versus ambulance-to-emergency assignment (Inference).** Patient allocation assigns demand to fixed, capacitated facilities and is dominated by knapsack-style capacity inequalities that need slack variables. Ambulance dispatch assigns mobile unit-capacity servers to demand points and, at this scale, is dominated by at-most-one constraints on both sides, which need no slack. The decisive difference is the after-effect: assigning a patient consumes capacity at a place that does not move, whereas dispatching an ambulance removes a server from a zone and changes spatial coverage of demand not yet observed. That externality is what the coverage term models and it has no direct analogue in the patient-allocation QUBOs seen. Dispatch is also a seconds-scale online decision, while the cited patient-allocation work is planning-level.

**Name and theme collisions.** A public repository `Sibi7Antony/Q-Rescue`, a "Quantum Disaster Response & Coordination Platform", already exposes QUBO and QAOA-simulation endpoints using Qiskit, Aer and Qiskit Optimization with "QAOA-inspired local simulation and graceful fallback", and its README disclaims quantum advantage ([GitHub](https://github.com/Sibi7Antony/Q-Rescue), README read, code not audited). Whether it belongs to this team could not be determined. A classical hackathon project, RESQAI, already implements "Emergency Fleet Assignment with Coverage Preservation" with a penalty for sending the last idle unit in a quadrant, scaled by incident priority, benchmarked against nearest-unit greedy ([GitHub](https://github.com/Devsuthar2006/ambulance), README read). Another hackathon repository uses a QAOA/VQE QUBO with an ambulance response-time risk term ([GitHub](https://github.com/Amaan-Hans/Grid-Aware-Emergency-Resource-Allocation), README read). "Quantum plus ambulance" will not look original on its own, and a judge who searches the project name will find the first repository.

It is technically meaningful only in its pairwise form, because that is the single ingredient that lifts the problem out of polynomial-time linear assignment; a linear penalty contributes nothing computationally. **Evidence needed to support even the narrow gap**: forward-citation checks of arXiv:2211.13080 and arXiv:2006.05846 and native searches of IEEE Xplore, ACM DL and Scopus; clarification of the same-name repository; instances, verified by enumeration, where the exact optimum with coverage differs from the exact optimum without it and from greedy; a rolling simulation showing whether the coverage-aware choice changes response to the next call; and QAOA results reported with feasibility rate, per-shot optimum probability, penalty sweep, multiple seeds and a random-sampling control. **Confidence**: high that ambulance QUBO/QAOA work pre-exists (full text read) and that the method stack is not methodologically new; moderate, roughly two in three by the researchers' subjective estimate, that no indexed English-language paper combines the two terms in a QUBO/QAOA formulation; lower for unindexed hackathon submissions and theses. **Unresolved**: ownership of the same-name repository, the contents of items 9 and 10, whether the Andhra Pradesh pilot's startups have published a dispatch formulation, and whether a classical paper already writes the multi-dispatch coverage loss as an explicit pairwise expansion.

In a targeted, non-exhaustive search we did not find a QUBO/QAOA formulation combining call assignment with a future-coverage term. At the sizes studied the problem is solved exactly and instantly by classical methods."

## D. Mathematical formulation: a reward-form assignment with an exact pair-coverage term

Everything in this section is Design or the researchers' own derivation unless a source is cited; all numbers are Computed by brute-force enumeration on synthetic illustration data. No QAOA was run.

### Variables, parameters and units

Let `A` be the set of currently idle ambulances (index `i`, also `a`, `b`) and `C` the set of pending calls (index `j`, `k`). `E ⊆ A×C` is the set of eligible pairs: capability matches (an ALS-required call only to an ALS unit), the unit is available, and optionally `t_ij ≤ T_max`. `t_ij` is the estimated travel time in minutes. `w_j` is a dimensionless priority weight, higher meaning more urgent. `τ` is the unserved-equivalent time in minutes: leaving call `j` unserved this round costs as much as a `τ`-minute response, so the unserved penalty is `U_j = w_j·τ` in weighted minutes. The decision variable `x_ij ∈ {0,1}` exists only for `(i,j) ∈ E` and equals 1 if ambulance `i` is assigned to call `j`. Derived quantities are the dispatch indicator `D_i = Σ_j x_ij` and the served indicator `s_j = Σ_i x_ij`, each 0 or 1 on feasible strings. For coverage, `Z` is a set of zones, `d_z ≥ 0` the expected demand rate of zone `z` in calls per hour, and `N_z ⊆ A` the idle ambulances able to reach `z` within a threshold `T` minutes. `λ` converts uncovered demand into weighted minutes and has units of weighted minutes per (call per hour). `P_A` and `P_C` are penalty weights in weighted minutes.

### Objective and weights

The natural cost is `F(x) = Σ_E w_j·t_ij·x_ij + Σ_j U_j·(1 − s_j) + λ·L(x)`: priority-weighted response time, plus a penalty for each unserved call, plus weighted residual-coverage loss. Because `1 − s_j` is the unserved indicator whenever `s_j ≤ 1`, expanding gives `F = Σ_j U_j + Σ_E (w_j·t_ij − U_j)·x_ij + λ·L(x)`. No unserved variable is needed, and an assignment is attractive exactly when `t_ij < τ`. `U_j` is a real modelling parameter that decides whether a slow assignment or a coverage loss is worth it, not a constraint constant. A principled starting value for `λ` is the unit conversion `λ = w̄·Δt·H`: an uncovered rate over `H` hours of unit unavailability yields expected calls, each incurring about `Δt` extra minutes at mean priority `w̄`; with `w̄ = 2`, `Δt = 10`, `H = 1` this gives `λ = 20`. All three factors are rough, so a `λ` sweep must be reported, not a single value. No source gives an empirically calibrated exchange rate between response minutes and coverage.

### Hard constraints

Each ambulance serves at most one call (`D_i ≤ 1`), each call receives at most one ambulance (`s_j ≤ 1`), and variables exist only for eligible pairs. There is deliberately no "serve every call" equality, which is what makes demand exceeding capacity unproblematic.

### The two future-coverage approaches

**Approach A, per-ambulance linear loss** `λ·Σ_i c_i·D_i`, where `c_i` is the demand that becomes uncovered if ambulance `i` alone leaves. It adds no couplings and is exactly the quantity used by the single-call Jagtenberg heuristic, which evaluates "the marginal coverage that each ambulance provides for the region" ([Jagtenberg et al. manuscript](https://ir.cwi.nl/pub/24632/24632A.pdf), V-full). Its flaw in a batch is that coverage loss is not additive. On the synthetic main instance, dispatching all three ambulances has true loss 2.6 calls per hour while the sum of marginals reports 0.5; two units that back each other up each look free to send, and together they strip the shared zone (Computed). Defining `c_i` instead as all demand in zones the ambulance participates in over-counts everything, reporting 2.1 for a single dispatch whose true loss is 0. With the marginal definition the model does not hold a unit back until `λ` exceeds about 90, against a true switch point near 10.6 (Computed). Approach A is also still a linear assignment problem.

**Approach B, post-dispatch zone coverage.** Zone `z` is uncovered after dispatch if and only if every unit in `N_z` has left, so `uncovered_z = Π_{i∈N_z} D_i` and `L = Σ_z d_z·Π_{i∈N_z} D_i`. This is a multilinear polynomial of degree exactly `|N_z|`; it is exactly quadratic if and only if every zone has at most two covering units, in which case `L = Σ_i c_i·D_i + Σ_{a<b} g_ab·D_a·D_b`, with `c_i` the demand only `i` covers and `g_ab` the demand covered only by the pair `{a,b}`. The cross term is precisely the correction approach A lacks, with no independence assumption and no double counting. For zones with three or four covering units, an exact reduction needs one auxiliary qubit per zone via Ishikawa's quadratization (formula taken from secondary sources because the original returned HTTP 403, corroborated by the researchers' enumeration for degree 3 to 7; see [arXiv:1404.6535](https://arxiv.org/abs/1404.6535)). The probabilistic alternative, MEXCLP, weights the k-th covering unit by `d(1−q)q^(k−1)` under a common busy fraction `q` ([Jagtenberg et al. 2015 preprint](https://ir.cwi.nl/pub/23338/23338B.pdf), V-full restatement of Daskin 1983, original not read); it assumes units are busy independently with identical probability, and its second-order truncation is non-monotone, claiming for `q = 0.3` that a third covering unit lowers coverage from 0.91 to 0.63 against a true 0.973 (Computed). Double-counting hazards are four: letting coverage "buy" future protection by leaving a present call unserved; estimating `d_z` from data that include the calls being assigned; applying `q` to units that have also been removed; and adding an approach-A term on top of the zone formula.

The two formulation notes disagree on framing, and the disagreement is worth stating. One derives the pairwise term from the MEXCLP expansion (probabilistic, coefficient `d(1−q)²q^(k−2)`), the other from deterministic single coverage (coefficient `g_ab`). Both conclude the same structural facts: linear for a single dispatch, genuinely pairwise once two dispatched units share a zone, higher-order beyond that. **The simplest defensible recommendation is the deterministic pair-coverage form with cover sets truncated to the two nearest idle units**, described openly as "coverage by one of the two nearest units". It is exact, needs no auxiliary qubits, carries no busy-fraction assumption, and can be checked zone by zone from raw data.

**Does a static snapshot support a "dynamic coverage" claim? No.** The model has no arrival process, no service completion, no relocation and no re-optimisation; `d_z` is a rate evaluated once for the post-dispatch state. The classical literature draws the same line, calling marginal coverage "an idea that originated in static ambulance planning" and evaluating any dynamic use by simulating the region over time ([Jagtenberg et al. 2015 preprint](https://ir.cwi.nl/pub/23338/23338B.pdf)). Supportable wording is "one-shot assignment with a penalty for forecast demand left without a unit within T minutes after dispatch (residual coverage)". Coverage proxies can also mislead: a preparedness-based dispatch rule was found to perform worse than closest-available ([arXiv:2203.16371](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.16371), secondary restatement of Lee 2011).

### The QUBO with explicit coefficients

Index variables by eligible pairs `e = (i,j)` and minimise `H(x) = Σ_e h_e·x_e + Σ_{e<f} J_ef·x_e·x_f`, dropping the constant `Σ_j U_j` so that `F = H + Σ_j U_j` on feasible strings. The linear coefficient is `h_ij = w_j·t_ij − U_j + λ·c_i`. The quadratic coefficient for two distinct variables `(a,j)` and `(b,k)` is `P_A` if they share an ambulance (`a = b`, `j ≠ k`); `P_C + λ·g_ab` if they share a call (`a ≠ b`, `j = k`); and `λ·g_ab` if they share neither. All couplings are non-negative; only linear terms are negative. The at-most-one constraints are enforced exactly by pairwise products `P·Σ_{a<b} x_a·x_b`, zero when feasible and at least `P` otherwise, with no slack qubits, which is the textbook form `x + y ≤ 1 → P(xy)` ([Glover, Kochenberger & Du, arXiv:1811.11538](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.11538), V-full; [Lucas 2014](https://arxiv.org/abs/1302.5843), V-full). An "exactly one" penalty `P(s_j − 1)²` without an unserved bit must be avoided: it silently equals the pairwise penalty at weight `2P` plus a service reward of `P` per served call, fusing a physical trade-off with a constraint weight. For the circuit, substitute `x_k = (1 − z_k)/2`, giving `J^Z_kl = J_kl/4`, `h^Z_k = −h_k/2 − ¼·Σ_{l≠k} J_kl` and offset `Σ_k h_k/2 + Σ_{k<l} J_kl/4`.

### Choosing and scaling penalties

A provable sufficient condition, when all coverage coefficients are non-negative and a single weight `P = P_A = P_C` is used, is **`P > max over eligible pairs of (U_j − w_j·t_ij)`**: clearing any bit in a violated group removes at least one penalty unit while the objective can worsen by at most that maximum (researchers' derivation, in the spirit of Lucas's `B < A` condition). Glover et al. warn that a penalty that is too large lets "the penalty terms overwhelm the original objective function information", while one too small "jeopardizes the search for feasible solutions" ([arXiv:1811.11538](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.11538)), and quadratic penalties introduce "large energy scales" that can degrade QAOA ([Mirkarimi et al., arXiv:2404.05467](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.05467), V-abstract). The guidance is therefore: set `P` just above the provable bound, never orders of magnitude above; divide all Ising coefficients by one positive scale before building the circuit; recompute costs from raw data rather than un-scaling sampled energies; and before any QAOA run, confirm by enumeration that the QUBO minimiser is feasible and equals the constrained optimum. If gadgets or mixed-sign coverage rewards are introduced the bound no longer holds and enumeration is mandatory.

### Unserved calls when demand exceeds capacity

The reward form handles this without extra variables: at most `min(|A|, |C|, maximum matching in E)` calls are served and the rest pay `U_j`. The classical literature uses four conventions for calls that cannot be served immediately, namely a first-come-first-served queue, a lost call, an outside responder, and explicit queue variables with waiting penalties ([arXiv:2203.16371](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.16371), V-full); Q-Rescue's is a one-round deferral with a priority-scaled penalty and must be stated as such. The uncomfortable consequence is that the coverage term can make deferral optimal, which the worked example shows.

### Worked example with verified numbers

**Hand-checkable instance, 2 ambulances × 3 calls, 5 qubits (synthetic; Computed).** Priorities `w = (3, 2, 1)`, `τ = 20`, so `U = (60, 40, 20)` and `ΣU = 120`. Travel times in minutes: A1 to C1, C2, C3 = 5, 8, 6; A2 to C2, C3 = 4, 9; A2 is ineligible for C1. Zones: Za covered by {A1}, 0.5 calls/h; Zb by {A1, A2}, 1.0; Zc by {A2}, 0.4; hence `c = (0.5, 0.4)` and `g_12 = 1.0`. Weights `λ = 10`, `P = 50`. Variables `x_0..x_4` = (A1,C1), (A1,C2), (A1,C3), (A2,C2), (A2,C3). Linear coefficients `h = (−40, −19, −9, −28, −7)`. Couplings: 50 on pairs (0,1), (0,2), (1,2), (3,4); 60 on (1,3), (2,4); 10 on (0,3), (0,4), (1,4), (2,3). Of 32 bitstrings 10 are feasible. The optimum is `10010`, A1→C1 and A2→C2, with `H = −40 − 28 + 10 = −58` and `F = 62`. The raw-data check agrees: response `3·5 + 2·4 = 23`, unserved C3 = 20, uncovered demand 1.9 calls/h times 10 = 19, total 62. The provable penalty bound here is `P > 45`. At `λ = 0` the optimum is the same assignment and equals greedy; for `λ` above 22.86 the optimum becomes A1→C1 only, deferring the priority-2 call.

**Main instance, 3 ambulances × 4 calls with four ineligible pairs, 8 qubits (synthetic; Computed).** With `λ = 20`, `P = 100`, there are 256 bitstrings, 27 feasible. The exact optimum is A1→C1, A3→C3 at total cost `F = 168` (weighted response 38, unserved penalty 120, uncovered 0.5 calls/h). Greedy nearest-available, which coincides with the `λ = 0` optimum, sends all three units at cost 186 (weighted response 44, unserved 90, uncovered 2.6 calls/h). The global QUBO minimum over all 256 strings equals the constrained optimum (−132), with the lowest infeasible string at −89. The provable penalty bound is 96; the enumerated threshold is 57 at `λ = 20` and 63 at `λ = 0`, which shows why a per-instance tuned threshold should not be trusted. **The 18-unit gain comes from leaving the lowest-priority call unserved and delaying another by 3 minutes to keep one ambulance idle.** For `λ` above 180 the model leaves the highest-priority call unserved. A cleaner variant with only two calls and no deferral (4 qubits) sends the second-nearest unit to one call, adding 3 minutes to keep 0.4 calls/h covered, at cost 48 versus greedy's 50, switching at `λ = 15`.

### Decoding, with the bit-order caveat

Fix one variable order, the eligible pairs sorted by ambulance then call, and map variable `k` to qubit `k`, with any auxiliary qubits last. Qiskit is little-endian: in a displayed bitstring "bit n-1 is the leftmost bit, and bit 0 is the rightmost bit" ([IBM bit-ordering guide](https://quantum.cloud.ibm.com/docs/en/guides/bit-ordering), verified in the Qiskit note), and IBM's own QAOA tutorial reverses the decoded list ([IBM QAOA tutorial](https://quantum.cloud.ibm.com/docs/en/tutorials/quantum-approximate-optimization-algorithm)). So for a counts key `s` of length `n`, `x_k = int(s[n − 1 − k])`. On the main instance the optimum `10000010` in variable order appears as Qiskit key `01000001`; reading it unreversed decodes to a different assignment that is **feasible but wrong**, so feasibility checks alone will not catch the bug (Computed). A basis-state test (X on qubit 0 only, assert the decoder returns the first eligible pair) is required, and the newer primitives' `BitArray` handling should be checked against the installed version.

### Independent validation of every decoded solution

Each decoded sample is validated from raw tables, never from the QUBO matrix: every selected pair is eligible; each ambulance appears at most once; each call appears at most once; weighted response and unserved penalty are recomputed; coverage is recomputed zone by zone from the remaining ambulances; the recomputed total must equal QUBO energy plus `ΣU` on feasible samples; policy rules are checked; and the result is compared with the exact optimum to report the gap. Infeasible samples are rejected and counted, not repaired, because a repair step is itself a classical optimiser that can make a near-uniform sampler look competent. Energies of infeasible strings are not costs and must never enter an average reported as objective value.

### Exact classical benchmark

Minimise `Σ_E w_j·t_ij·x_ij + Σ_j U_j·(1 − Σ_i x_ij) + λ·Σ_z d_z·v_z` subject to `Σ_j x_ij ≤ 1` for all `i`, `Σ_i x_ij ≤ 1` for all `j`, `v_z ≥ Σ_{i∈N_z} D_i − (|N_z| − 1)` for every zone with a non-empty cover set, `x_ij ∈ {0,1}` and `0 ≤ v_z ≤ 1`. Because demand is non-negative and the problem minimises, `v_z` settles at the product of the dispatch indicators, so this linearisation is exact for any cover-set size. At prototype size, direct enumeration of all bitstrings is the simplest exact solver and also yields the feasible-set statistics needed for the other metrics. On complexity: with `λ = 0` or approach A the problem is polynomial; with pair-coverage terms it is NP-hard in the worst case by the researchers' own reduction sketch from Independent Set, which is not from a published source and should be labelled as such. At 3×4 it is trivial.

### Safety rules that must be hard, not penalties

Capability, availability and maximum allowable response time are structural: no variable is created for an ineligible pair, so no sample can represent a BLS unit sent to an ALS-only call. The two at-most-one families are penalty-encoded but must also be enforced by post-hoc validation with rejection, because penalty encodings can leave violating states near or below the optimum ([Montañez-Barrera et al., arXiv:2211.13914](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.13914), V-full via ar5iv). Top-priority calls must never be deferred for coverage: pre-assign them classically to the closest eligible unit and remove them from the QUBO, mirroring the practice of restricting coverage-aware dispatch to lower priorities ([arXiv:2203.16371](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.16371), secondary restatement of Andersson & Värbrand), and additionally cap `λ` so that `λ·(c_i + Σ_b g_ib) < U_j − w_j·t_ij`. The quantum routine's output is advisory only, with fallback to the exact classical result when validation fails, and any deferral must be displayed explicitly.

It is now read-only." ([GitHub](https://github.com/qiskit-community/qiskit-optimization)). **qiskit-algorithms 0.4.0** carries the same unsupported notice ([GitHub](https://github.com/qiskit-community/qiskit-algorithms)) and is not needed. IBM's suggested successor, **qiskit-addon-opt-mapper 0.1.0**, does modelling and mapping only and contains no QAOA ([docs](https://qiskit.github.io/qiskit-addon-opt-mapper/)). **Streamlit 1.65.0** was released 2 October 2026 ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/pypi/streamlit/1.65.0/json)). Python 3.12 satisfies every package's metadata; this is derived from metadata, not from a tested install, and no official statement was found that qiskit-optimization 0.7.0 was tested against Qiskit 2.5.

**Is Qiskit Optimization's QAOA interface appropriate?** It works on paper, since declared version ranges overlap, and it is the shortest path to a first result, with `QuadraticProgram`, automatic penalties and interpretation back to original variables. It is not appropriate as the foundation: the repository is archived, so a bug found during the hackathon will not be fixed, and its release history includes ordering bugs in exactly the places that matter here ([release notes](https://qiskit-community.github.io/qiskit-optimization/release_notes.html)). Its automatic penalty also falls back to `1e5` when constraint coefficients are non-integer ([source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/qiskit-community/qiskit-optimization/main/qiskit_optimization/converters/linear_equality_to_penalty.py)), which would swamp the objective; Q-Rescue's 0/1 constraint coefficients should not trigger it, but the penalty actually used must be printed.

**Recommended route (Design).** Build the QUBO matrix by hand, convert to Ising, construct the cost operator with `SparsePauliOp.from_sparse_list`, build the circuit with the function `qaoa_ansatz(cost_operator, reps=p)`, optimise against exact expectation values from `StatevectorEstimator`, then sample the optimised circuit with a seeded `StatevectorSampler` or Aer `SamplerV2`. This follows the current IBM tutorial pattern ([IBM QAOA tutorial](https://quantum.cloud.ibm.com/docs/en/tutorials/quantum-approximate-optimization-algorithm)) and depends only on supported packages. Use the function form, not the class: the Qiskit 2.1 release notes say all `QuantumCircuit` subclasses are deprecated for removal in 3.0 ([2.1 notes](https://quantum.cloud.ibm.com/docs/en/api/qiskit/release-notes/2.1)), yet the 2.5 API page and source for `QAOAAnsatz` show no deprecation notice and the tutorial still imports it ([QAOAAnsatz API](https://quantum.cloud.ibm.com/docs/en/api/qiskit/qiskit.circuit.library.QAOAAnsatz)); the sources conflict and the function sidesteps the question. qiskit-optimization may be used only as an optional cross-check of the hand-built Ising conversion. Tutorials importing `qiskit.algorithms`, `qiskit.opflow`, `QuantumInstance` or `execute` are obsolete for this stack.

**Classical optimiser.** COBYLA through `scipy.optimize.minimize`, as in the IBM tutorial. In exact statevector simulation "there is no significant difference in the performance of classical optimizers" ([Pellow-Jarman et al., arXiv:2307.10149](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.10149), V-abstract); if the team instead optimises on sampled estimates, shot noise changes that and SPSA is the safer choice. Random initialisation "is prone to converging to local minima with sub-optimal performance" ([Sack & Serbyn, arXiv:2101.05742](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.05742), V-abstract), so use a linear-ramp start, seed depth p+1 from the interpolated depth-p optimum, and run a handful of seeded random restarts as a control. Sweep p = 1, 2, 3 and report all three.

**Start at 3×4 or smaller? Smaller.** Begin with the 5-qubit hand-checkable 2×3 instance, where every energy can be verified on paper, then the 4-qubit two-call variant and the 8-qubit main instance. Qubit counts (researchers' arithmetic): pairwise penalties with ineligible pairs eliminated need exactly `|E|` qubits, so a fully eligible 3×4 needs **12**; an explicit unserved variable per call raises it to 16; slack or idle variables on every constraint raise it to 19; and keeping ineligible pairs as penalised variables wastes qubits and allows capability violations to be sampled. One-hot equality encodings therefore cost `|C|` extra qubits for no benefit, auxiliary variables are needed only for zones with three or more covering units (one each), and penalties cost no qubits but add couplings: on the main instance the coverage term raises couplings from 14 to 22 of a possible 28. Extra qubits are not free in quality terms either; one study reports p = 1 QAOA success probability falling from 0.227% to 0.02% on a 4-city TSP when slack qubits enlarged the search space ([arXiv:2211.13914](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.13914)). Memory is irrelevant at these sizes, since a statevector costs 16 bytes times 2^n ([Aer docs](https://qiskit.github.io/qiskit-aer/stubs/qiskit_aer.AerSimulator.html)), about 64 KiB at 12 qubits. No citable wall-clock benchmark exists for these sizes; the team must time its own runs.

**Compatibility of the coverage term with a quadratic objective.** Fully compatible when every zone has at most two covering units; otherwise truncate cover sets to the two nearest units and say so, or add one auxiliary qubit per three-cover zone. Do not use a second-order truncation of MEXCLP.

**Simulation settings and shots.** Two stages: optimise on exact expectations so the optimiser sees a deterministic objective; then sample a fixed, seeded number of shots. Official material uses 1,024 as the default and 10,000 for final sampling ([IBM QAOA tutorial](https://quantum.cloud.ibm.com/docs/en/tutorials/quantum-approximate-optimization-algorithm)); 1,024 to 8,192 is defensible provided the same number is used for the random baseline. At these sizes also compute the exact output distribution from the statevector and report exact probabilities. Shot counts must be read against search-space size: with 12 qubits and one optimal string, uniform random sampling finds the optimum at least once in 1,024 shots about 22% of the time and in 8,192 shots about 86% (researchers' arithmetic); at 8 qubits the same calculation gives about 98% at 1,024 shots (report writer's arithmetic from the same formula). "QAOA's best sample was optimal" is therefore nearly meaningless; the per-shot probability of the optimum is the honest metric. Fix and log seeds for the sampler, transpiler, initial point and instance generation. Omit a noise model in Round 1.

**When QAOA returns infeasible or poor results.** This should be expected, not treated as failure. On an ambulance-location QUBO, an X mixer with penalty gave only 0.10–0.15 optimum probability at p = 3 and 5 where a constraint-preserving mixer gave nearly 1 ([Cumming & Thomas, arXiv:2211.13080](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.13080), V-full), and penalty-based QAOA's ability "diminishes notably" on constrained routing ([Xie et al., arXiv:2308.08785](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.08785), V-abstract). The response is to classify every sample against the original constraints; report feasibility rate; report three decoders side by side (most-probable string, best feasible sample, expectation); record a failed run when no feasible sample appears and show the exact classical solution explicitly labelled as fallback; sweep the penalty over two or three values near the provable bound; and never repair silently. An XY-mixer variant is a labelled stretch experiment only: Q-Rescue has two overlapping constraint families on the same variables, a mixer preserving one does not preserve the other, and Qiskit ships no ready-made XY mixer or W-state initialiser in the pages read.

**Repeated stochastic runs.** Run 20 to 30 independent optimisations per instance, depth and configuration with distinct seeds (a statistical-practice recommendation, not a cited standard), report median and interquartile range, and show every run as a point.

**Limits of the simulator experiment.** A 4-to-12-qubit noiseless simulation shows that the formulation is correct and characterises a heuristic. It says nothing about speed-up, hardware, or scaling. Published work places any plausible QAOA speed-up for Max-Cut at "several hundreds of qubits" ([Guerreschi & Matsuura, arXiv:1812.07589](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.07589), V-abstract), and benchmark classes become hard for classical methods at sizes "ranging from less than 100" variables upward ([Koch et al., arXiv:2504.03832](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.03832), V-abstract). Because 4,096 states enumerate in well under a second while each QAOA run needs hundreds of circuit simulations, the simulated QAOA will be slower than brute force at every size in scope, and the write-up must say so.

## F. Evaluation design: the exact optimum is the reference, random sampling is the control

**Baselines.** Every method receives the identical objective, constraints and penalty-free definition of feasibility. The set is: (1) greedy nearest-available, calls in descending priority, nearest eligible idle unit; (2) linear assignment on the coverage-free costs via `linear_sum_assignment`, a "myopic optimal" baseline that isolates what the quadratic term buys; (3) exact classical optimum by enumeration, the ground truth; (4) QAOA at p = 1, 2, 3; (5) uniform random bitstrings at the same shot count, plus uniform random feasible assignments; and optionally (6) simulated annealing on the same QUBO using `dwave-samplers`, since `dwave-neal` is deprecated ([PyPI dwave-neal](https://pypi.org/pypi/dwave-neal/json)).

**Metrics.** For the dispatch decision: mean response time and priority-weighted response time over served calls; critical-call service rate; number and summed priority of unserved calls; post-dispatch uncovered demand in calls per hour; and total objective value recomputed from raw data. For the sampler: feasibility rate; per-shot probability of the optimum; optimality gap of the best feasible sample; approximation ratio on feasible samples with its definition stated; number of circuit evaluations; and wall-clock runtime including the optimisation loop and all restarts. Report an effective ratio normalised so uniform random sampling scores 0 and perfect sampling of the optimum scores 1.

**Avoiding bias toward QAOA.** Generate and freeze the scenario suite before any tuning. Use the same shot count and seed list for QAOA and the random control. Disclose the full tuning budget for every method. Never report only the best depth, best seed or best penalty. Never report "best sample found" without the same statistic for random sampling, because a maximum over N draws is biased upward with N. Never apply repair to one method only. Compare against the exact optimum, not only greedy. Published guidance supports each of these: benchmarking needs "clear metrics to conduct appropriate comparisons with classical optimization techniques" ([Abbas et al., arXiv:2312.02279](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.02279), V-abstract), and a result is meaningful only while distinguishable from random sampling ([Montañez-Barrera et al., arXiv:2502.06471](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.06471), V-abstract).

**Scenario suite (Design; all synthetic, all seeded).** Six to ten small scenarios are enough for Round 1. An easy case with no shared zones, where greedy, linear assignment and the exact optimum should coincide. A sanity case at `λ = 0`, where enumeration must equal linear assignment. The 2×3 hand-checkable case. A backup-pair case with more ambulances than calls, where the coverage-aware optimum sends the second-nearest unit and no call is deferred; this is the headline demonstration. A scarcity case, 3 ambulances and 4 calls, where coverage can act only through deferral, shown explicitly as a policy trade-off. A severity case with one top-priority call pre-assigned outside the QUBO. A travel-time asymmetry case where the nearest unit to one call is the only eligible unit for another. A high-coverage-risk case with one zone holding most of the demand behind a two-unit backup pair. And a fully eligible 3×4 at 12 qubits as the upper size. Each is run across a small `λ` sweep.

**Hypotheses, labelled as such; none has been tested.** *Hypothesis 1:* with the provable penalty, the QUBO minimiser equals the constrained optimum on every scenario (already confirmed by enumeration on three synthetic instances only). *Hypothesis 2:* penalty-encoded X-mixer QAOA at p ≤ 3 samples the optimum with per-shot probability above the uniform rate of 1/2^n but well below 1, with partial infeasibility. *Hypothesis 3:* QAOA feasibility rate exceeds the uniform-random feasible fraction, which is 27/256, about 10.5%, on the main instance. *Hypothesis 4:* results vary materially with the penalty weight. *Hypothesis 5:* greedy differs from the exact optimum only in scenarios with shared sole-backup zones and `λ` above an instance-specific breakpoint. Any of these may be refuted.

**What counts as success even if QAOA does not outperform classical methods.** The exact solver will match or beat QAOA on every instance; that is expected and is not failure. A successful prototype is one in which the QUBO is verified against enumeration on every scenario; at least one scenario shows, with the exact solver confirming it, that the coverage-aware optimum differs from greedy because of the pairwise term; QAOA demonstrably executes real circuits with stated depth, shots, optimiser and seeds; feasibility rate and per-shot optimum probability are reported honestly beside the random control; every decoded solution passes independent validation; and all of it is reproducible from pinned versions and seeds. A neutral or negative QAOA result, clearly reported, is a legitimate finding. What Round 1 cannot show is operational benefit: by the standards of the field that needs a rolling discrete-event simulation against closest-idle and a classical coverage-aware rule, reporting both threshold and mean response metrics, because the two trade off ([Jagtenberg et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10729-016-9368-0)).

## G. Final problem statement and contribution

**Formal problem statement.** Given a set of idle ambulances with capability types, a set of simultaneous pending calls with priority weights and capability requirements, an eligibility relation, estimated travel times for eligible pairs, and a set of demand zones each with an expected call rate and a set of at most two covering ambulances, choose a partial one-to-one assignment of ambulances to calls that minimises the sum of priority-weighted response time, priority-scaled penalties for calls left unserved in this round, and a weighted measure of zone demand left without any covering ambulance after dispatch; encode this as a QUBO over eligible-pair variables with pairwise at-most-one penalties, sample it with QAOA on a noiseless simulator, and validate every decoded assignment against an exact classical optimum.

**The 20-second sentence.** "When several emergencies arrive at once, sending the nearest ambulance to each can strip a neighbourhood of its only backup; we write that trade-off as one small optimisation problem, run it through a quantum algorithm on a simulator, and check every answer against the exact classical solution."

**Proposed contribution.** A verified QUBO formulation of multi-call ambulance assignment whose pairwise residual-coverage term is exact for two-unit cover sets and needs no slack or auxiliary qubits, together with a transparent benchmark harness (exact enumeration, greedy, linear assignment, random sampling, QAOA) and an independent validation layer. It is a formulation and benchmarking exercise at toy scale.

**Claims not to make.**

- That Q-Rescue is the first quantum approach to ambulance dispatch or emergency response, or that the idea is novel.
- That coverage-aware dispatch is a new concept.
- That QAOA outperforms, matches at scale, or is faster than classical optimisation.
- "Quantum advantage" in any form for a simulator run.
- That it "runs on a quantum computer".
- That the system reduces response times, saves lives or improves outcomes.
- "Dynamic", "real-time" or "predictive", unless a simulation or forecaster actually exists.
- That it scales to real cities.
- That it uses real Tamil Nadu 108 data, until a file is in hand.
- That the NAS 2026 guidelines require or recommend this approach.
- "No prior work exists"; say "not found in our search".

## H. Round 1 minimum viable prototype: a notebook-scale specification

This is a specification only. The verified Round 1 deliverables are slides, video, prototype and repository ([Submission page](https://quantumweek.tech/hackathon/submission)); a Jupyter notebook with runnable quantum circuits is an accepted prototype format.

**Required files and modules**

- Pinned requirements file with exact versions from section E, and a recorded Python version.
- Scenario definitions as data files: ambulances with type, calls with priority and required type, travel-time table, zones with rate and cover set, and the parameters `τ`, `λ`, `P`.
- Instance module: loads a scenario, builds the sorted eligible-pair list and the variable-to-qubit map.
- Coverage module: computes `c_i`, `g_ab` and, independently, uncovered demand zone by zone for a given set of dispatched units.
- QUBO module: builds `h` and `J` from the instance; converts to Ising coefficients and offset.
- Classical module: brute-force enumeration, greedy nearest-available, linear assignment, uniform random sampling.
- QAOA module: cost operator, ansatz, exact-expectation optimisation loop, seeded sampling, exact output distribution.
- Decoder and validator module: the single shared decode function and the raw-data validation checklist from section D.
- Experiment runner: executes all methods on all scenarios over seeds and writes one results file with parameters, seeds, versions, counts and metrics.
- One demonstration notebook that runs end to end and displays the circuit, the convergence curve, the sample histogram and the comparison table.
- README with setup instructions, the problem statement, the contribution statement, limitations, and the list of claims not made.

**Inputs.** Synthetic scenarios only, clearly labelled synthetic; depth p; shot count; seed list; `λ` sweep values; penalty values.

**Outputs.** Per scenario and method: the decoded assignment in plain language, including any deferred call named explicitly; mean and priority-weighted response time; unserved calls and their priorities; uncovered demand; total cost; feasibility rate; per-shot optimum probability; optimality gap; runtime. A single table placing greedy, linear assignment, exact, random and QAOA side by side.

**Tests**

- Ising energy plus offset equals QUBO value on every bitstring of the 5-qubit instance.
- QUBO value plus `ΣU` equals raw-data cost on every feasible bitstring.
- The QUBO global minimum equals the enumerated constrained optimum for each scenario at the chosen penalty.
- The hand instance reproduces `H = −58`, `F = 62` for assignment A1→C1, A2→C2.
- Basis-state bit-order test for the decoder.
- At `λ = 0`, enumeration matches linear assignment.
- The validator rejects a hand-constructed infeasible string and a hand-constructed ineligible pair cannot be represented.

**Demonstration steps, mapped to the three-minute video.** State the problem with the backup-pair scenario; show greedy's assignment and the zone left uncovered; show the exact coverage-aware optimum and what it costs in minutes; show the QUBO size and the circuit; execute QAOA live on the smallest instance or show the recorded run with its seeds; show the sample histogram with feasibility rate and optimum probability next to the random control; show the validator passing; close with the limitations sentence. Narration must be by team members.

**Mapping to deliverables.** Prototype: the notebook and modules. Repository: all files above, public. Video: the demonstration steps. Slides: problem, formulation, prior work and honest positioning, method, results table, limitations, roadmap, on the official template.

## Final recommendation

**Build first** the 5-qubit hand-checkable instance end to end: scenario, enumeration, QUBO, Ising conversion with its exhaustive energy test, decoder with its bit-order test, then one p = 1 QAOA run with a random-sampling control. Only when that chain reproduces the known optimum should the team move to the backup-pair demonstration scenario and the 8-qubit instance. Everything else is secondary to that chain being correct.

**The quantum part is justified only narrowly.** The pairwise coverage term makes the problem a genuine quadratic binary optimisation rather than a relabelled linear assignment, and its at-most-one constraints encode without slack qubits, so it is a natural and compact QUBO and a legitimate test problem for QAOA. That is the whole justification. At every size in scope a classical exact solver returns the optimum instantly, the simulated QAOA will be slower and less reliable, and nothing in a noiseless simulation speaks to hardware or scaling. The honest position is a validated, quantum-ready formulation with a transparent benchmark.

**What is distinctive, if anything,** is modest: in a targeted and non-exhaustive search, no QUBO/QAOA formulation was found that combines multi-call assignment with a quadratic residual-coverage term and checks it against exact enumeration. The concept is old, the toolchain is standard, and the name is already in use elsewhere. The most credible differentiator available to a student team in a field crowded with emergency-themed QAOA projects is rigour: an exact reference, a random control, an independent validator and a plainly stated list of limits.

**What must be validated before implementation begins:** that the team's coverage term is truly pairwise; that the penalty bound makes the QUBO minimiser equal the constrained optimum on every scenario; that the decoder's bit order is correct; that the deadline and prototype requirement are as read; and that the same-name repository is accounted for. One finding deserves to change how the team thinks about its own idea: on the proposed 3-ambulance, 4-call sizing, the coverage term earns its keep only by leaving a caller waiting. A project that shows that trade-off openly, rather than presenting coverage protection as a free improvement, is both more defensible and more interesting than the one originally proposed.
